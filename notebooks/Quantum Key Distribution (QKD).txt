# Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)

## Overview
Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) is a quantum cybersecurity technology that enables secure exchange of encryption keys using quantum mechanics. Its security comes from the fact that any attempt to intercept quantum information introduces detectable disturbances.

---

## Why QKD Matters

Current encryption methods (RSA, ECC) may be vulnerable to future quantum computers through algorithms like **Shor's Algorithm**.

Main threat:

> "Harvest now, decrypt later" — attackers collect encrypted data today and decrypt it in the future.

QKD provides a physics-based security approach.

---

## How QKD Works

**Example: BB84 Protocol**

1. Sender encodes information into quantum states (usually photons).
2. Receiver measures the quantum states.
3. Both parties compare measurements through a classical channel.
4. Interception attempts create errors that reveal attackers.
5. A shared secure key is generated.

---

## Main QKD Technologies

### Discrete Variable QKD (DV-QKD)
- Uses single photons and polarization states.
- Examples: BB84, E91.
- Most mature approach.

### Continuous Variable QKD (CV-QKD)
- Uses properties of light fields.
- Compatible with existing optical networks.

### Measurement-Device-Independent QKD (MDI-QKD)
- Reduces vulnerabilities from measurement devices.

### Satellite QKD
- Uses satellites for long-distance quantum communication.

## Key Challenges

- Limited communication distance due to photon loss.
- High infrastructure cost.
- Hardware side-channel vulnerabilities.
- Requires classical authentication.

---

## Applications

- Government and defense communication
- Financial security
- Critical infrastructure protection
